# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Culturally curious people planning a city trip — with or without a fixed anchor event. Three modes, one product:

- **The open traveller**: flexible dates, no fixed plans. Wants AnyWent to tell them where to go and when. High taste signal (Spotify-connected), trusts the score.
- **The anchor tripper**: already going somewhere for a specific event (a concert, a show). Wants to know what else is worth doing that same weekend — galleries, openings, a club night after the gig.
- **The local planner**: city is fixed, dates are loose. Browsing what's culturally worth leaving home for in the next few weeks.

The unifying trait: they care about culture in a wide sense — club nights and jazz sets and museum exhibitions and gallery openings — and they want a personal signal, not a trending feed. For now, single user (M). Design scales to multiple users with different taste profiles.

## Product Purpose

AnyWent aggregates the full cultural calendar across European cities — concerts, club nights, exhibitions, museum shows, gallery openings, festivals — and surfaces the best windows to travel, ranked by the user's own taste.

It is not a ticketing platform. It is not a discovery feed. It is a trusted recommendation from someone who knows: "Go to Berlin this weekend. Here's why."

Success looks like: the user books a trip they wouldn't have known to book, or fills a city weekend with things they genuinely loved.

## Brand Personality

Cultured · Insider · Unhurried.

The voice is a well-travelled local friend — the one who's been to Berghain enough times it's not a big deal, who knows which gallery opening has the good wine, who sends a voice note at 11pm saying "there's a thing tonight." Knowledgeable, unperformative. Recommends with confidence. Doesn't push.

Genre-agnostic: equally at home with a Sunday-morning techno set and a Wednesday afternoon vernissage. The taste engine is the filter, not the category.

## Anti-references

- **Eventbrite / Ticketmaster** — transactional, commerce-first, noisy. Feels like a marketplace.
- **Spotify (app)** — algorithmic, mainstream, green-and-pop aesthetic. Too much machine, not enough editorial.
- **TripAdvisor / Google Maps** — tourist-facing, review-aggregator, untrusted crowd. The opposite of insider.
- **Generic SaaS dark mode** — blue-tinted card grids that could be any B2B tool. No cultural identity.
- **RA event listings** — dense and credible, but genre-siloed (electronic only) and impersonal. AnyWent is broader and more personal.

## Design Principles

1. **The insider over the algorithm** — recommendations feel personal, not computed. The score means something because the taste signal is yours.
2. **Confidence without insistence** — the interface has opinions and shows them clearly. It does not upsell, nudge, or explain itself.
3. **Culture is the point** — events are experiences to anticipate, not transactions to complete. The UI reflects that weight.
4. **Dark rooms welcome** — the aesthetic earns the content. Berghain and a Rothko exhibition both live in dark, considered spaces. The interface lives there too.
5. **Taste is the filter, not the genre** — the same product serves the techno connoisseur and the family going to Harry Styles. What differs is the taste signal, not the interface.
6. **Show, don't explain** — let the score and the lineup speak. Minimal chrome around the data. No tooltips explaining what affinity means.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as the floor. Standard contrast (≥4.5:1 body, ≥3:1 large text) and keyboard navigation throughout. No specific known user needs beyond this. Dark theme is the primary — ensure it holds at AA, not just visually.
